# Batch 13 — Stadt, Ämter & Recht · Szenenplan

140 target words. **Fourteen scenes**, four to sixteen words each, all in **October, Y3**.
slots.md and continuity.md both use this year label now. The month is busy with authorities:
a house meeting, a phone scam and the police, a parking ticket, a morning at the Rathaus,
a visa, the trial of February's cellar burglars, a strike, a camera, a politician and a
local legend.

The topic has three hazards:

- **Paperwork recited as a list.** Antrag, Formular, Unterschrift, Stempel, Dokument and
  Urkunde could fill one counter scene. They are split over three settings, and each
  setting has a stake. At the Rathaus, Tobi has never registered his own flat (Texts 5–6).
  The Sattlers need a visa for Jan's parents (Text 7). Bruckner's Urkunde comes with a
  coded Zeugnis (Text 10).
- **Crime-film register.** The corpus leans towards crime. Here every crime word is said
  by someone ordinary in the house. Frau Wolf is the scam target and later the witness.
  Bruckner admires the burglar who fixed his door. Neumann films his own smoking.
- **Wrong legal facts.** A learner memorises the facts along with the words, so they are
  checked:
  - Not registering an address is not allowed and costs a fine. It is not a crime, so the
    Beamtin says *"Erlaubt ist es nicht … es kostet etwas"* (Text 5).
  - A private person reports a parked car to the Amt, and the Amt writes the Strafzettel
    (Text 4).
  - For the visa, a Behörde here confirms that the hosts will pay, and the guests apply at
    the consulate in their own country (Text 7).
  - Real police never collect valuables (Text 2).
  - A young offender gets hours of work in a social organisation with a social worker
    (Text 9).
  - Bruckner's *"bestimmt strafbar"* about the camera is his own guess, and Julia
    questions it in the next turn (Text 12).

## Sense decisions

- **`die Anzeige`** is a report to the police, CSV sense 2 (*"sonst bekommen Sie eine
  Anzeige"*). It is not a newspaper advert. Text 3.
- **`die Messe`** is a trade fair, as in the CSV (*Automobilmesse*). It is never a church
  mass. Text 11: *"und dazu ist auch noch Messe"*.
- **`die Ursache`** is *"Keine Ursache!"*, CSV sense 2, said by the policeman when Julia
  thanks him (Text 11). Its partner `verursachen` takes the causal sense in the same text.
- **`das Zeugnis`** is an employment reference (*Arbeitszeugnis*). The CSV gives an exam
  certificate, so this is the same word in another context. Text 10. The exam sense would
  touch Batch 15's school slot.
- **`das Zertifikat`** is Neumann's course certificate (Text 12).
- **`die Urkunde`** is an honorary certificate for 21 years of service (Text 10). This is
  close to the CSV's *Siegerurkunde*.
- **`der Dienst`** means years of service: *"Für einundzwanzig Jahre treuen Dienst"*
  (Text 10).
- **`schuldig`** is the verdict (*"Schuldig, alle beide"*, Text 9), not the CSV's *"Was
  bin ich Ihnen schuldig?"*.
- **`die Schulden`** are the young burglar's debts (Text 9). **`die Schuld`** is Tobi's
  *"nicht meine Schuld"* (Text 4), and **`schuld`** is Julia's *"bist du schuld"*
  (Text 11).
- **`der Doktor`** is a form of address for the lawyer, *"Herr Doktor"* (Text 8). He is not
  a physician.
- **`festsetzen`** is the CSV sense: the Chefin *"hat diese Versammlung festgesetzt"*
  (Text 1).
- **`der Personenstand` is skipped.** The ledger's forms column itself says *Familienstand*
  is the D/A term. German forms print *Familienstand*, and *Personenstand* is register
  jargon that nobody says aloud.

## Scene order is ownership order, with scanner artefacts

These scanner behaviours were found by a probe before writing:

- **`angeben`** is matched by any *gibt* or *Geben*. **`festsetzen`** is matched by *setzt*,
  and **`bekannt geben`** by *bekannt*. Texts 1–4 avoid *gibt*, and Texts 1–10 avoid
  *bekannt*.
- **The participle *eingebrochen* is not matched.** *Einbrecher* credits both
  `der Einbrecher` and `einbrechen`, so Text 2 owns both. Text 8 no longer needs the verb.
- ***Dienstausweis*, *Sozialarbeiter* and *Drogerie* credit nothing beyond their own
  headword.** So Text 3 can show the police ID without taking `der Ausweis`.

These artefacts are fixed with manual `use` rows after `scan --apply`. The automatic row
comes back on every rescan and is ignored, and the card is cut from the owner text:

| word | scanner sees it first in | owner text (card) | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `(sich) streiten` | 1 (*Streit*) | **6** *"Jetzt streiten wir uns bitte nicht im Rathaus"* | the noun is read as the verb |
| `anzeigen` | 3 (*Anzeige*) | **4** *"Ich habe Sie nur angezeigt"* | the noun is read as the verb |
| `die Schulden`, `schuld` | 4 (*Schuld*) | **9** *"Der Junge hatte Schulden"* / **11** *"bist du schuld"* | all three share one stem |
| `das Recht` | 11 (*Rechts vor links*) | **12** *"Jeder Mieter hat das Recht"* | *rechts* is read as *Recht* |
| `der Herr` | 1 (*Herr Neumann*) | **8** *"der Herr Anwalt"* | titles credit the noun; no manual row needed, carding note only |

`vocab.py use --batch 13 --text 11 "schuld"` resolves to `die Schuld`, not `schuld`. So the
`schuld` row (text 11) was inserted directly with sqlite
(`insert into uses(word_id,batch,text_no,source) values(<id of schuld>,13,11,'manual')`), and
the wrong `die Schuld` text-11 row was deleted. The other four manual rows came from `use`.
