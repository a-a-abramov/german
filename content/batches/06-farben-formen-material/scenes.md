# Batch 6 — Farben, Formen & Material · Szenenplan

41 target words, and the batch with the **highest adjective share so far**: 24 of the 41
are adjectives, most of them one half of an antonym pair (`hart`/`weich`, `hell`/`dunkel`,
`laut`/`leise`, `breit`/`schmal`, `senkrecht`/`waagerecht`). Only 17 words are nouns.

**Seven scenes, not the skill's 8–14** — the same call as Batch 5, for the same reason: 41
words over twelve dialogues is three targets per text, which is the word list driving the
structure again. Seven scenes of 4–8 words each keep every dialogue at six to eight turns
and leave room for the glue pool.

## Antonym pairs are the one grouping this batch *wants*

The skill's synonym rule says two names for one thing must never share a dialogue. Its
converse holds just as strongly here: an antonym pair is exactly what a speaker reaches
for in one breath, because the contrast *is* the utterance — *"Vorher war er weich, jetzt
ist er hart."* All five pairs are therefore deliberately kept together, one pair per scene:

| pair | scene | the contrast that carries it |
|---|:-:|---|
| `breit` / `schmal` | 1 | the table is wider than the staircase |
| `hart` / `weich` | 2 | what a 60-degree wash did to the wool |
| `senkrecht` / `waagerecht` | 4 | the two lines on the courtyard plan |
| `laut` / `leise` | 6 | before and after the new windows |
| `hell` / `dunkel` | 7 | the hallway has no window and Julia bought the dark tin |

## Sense decisions made against the CSV, not against the corpus

Three words in this batch mean something in the Goethe list that the film corpus does not
lead with. The CSV wins in all three:

- **`der Bogen` is a sheet of paper**, per the CSV's only example (*"Bitte schreiben Sie
  Ihren Namen auf den Antwortbogen"*). The corpus knows only the bow you shoot with
  (`mit dem Bogen schießen`, 29) and the arc you fly in (`einen weiten Bogen machen`, 58).
  Scene 4 uses the paper sense, and uses the bare noun in at least one line — the
  scanner's folded stems match `Bogen` inside `Fragebogen`, so a text that only ever says
  *Fragebogen* would score coverage while teaching the compound. The word **Formular** is
  banned from this batch for the same reason: it contains `Form`.
- **`der Kreis` is a drawn circle**, not the administrative district (*Kreis Neuss*) and
  not the social circle the corpus prefers (`im engen Kreis`, 218; `im Kreis der Familie`,
  152). Scene 4 has Frau Wolf draw one — `einen Kreis malen` (35) is attested.
- **`das Kreuz` is a mark in a box**, per the CSV (*"Was bedeutet dieses Kreuz hier?"* —
  the line Scene 4 ends on). The corpus's `Kreuz` is overwhelmingly the religious one
  (`am Kreuz sterben`, 116) and the `Rote Kreuz` (895); neither is written.

Two more senses chosen deliberately, both CSV-backed: **`rein` is *pure*** (*"aus reiner
Wolle"*), never the spoken short form of *herein* — no line in this batch says *komm rein*
or *reinkommen*, which would take the coverage credit and teach the wrong word. And
**`glatt` is *smooth to the touch*** (the wool before the wash), not the icy road, which
would need a season this batch doesn't have.

## Synonym / near-pair check

| pair | kind | scenes | ruling |
|---|---|:-:|---|
| `das Plastik` / `der Kunststoff` | true synonyms | 5 / 6 | **split** — the batch's one genuinely interchangeable pair. Plastik is the fake leather in a parcel; Kunststoff is what window frames are made of, which is the register that word actually lives in |
| `bunt` / `farbig` | near-synonyms | 7 / 4 | **split** — `farbig` is the CSV's *farbiges Papier* (coloured, i.e. not white), `bunt` is many-coloured. Adjacent they invite self-glossing |
| `der Stoff` / `das Material` | near-synonyms | 2 / 5 | **split** — `Stoff` is cloth in the laundry scene (*"Was für ein Stoff ist das? – Wolle"*), `Material` is the general question in the parcel scene (*"Aus welchem Material ist das?"*) |
| `eng` / `schmal` | near-pair | 2 / 1 | **split** — `eng` is a garment that no longer fits, `schmal` is a staircase. Confusable enough to keep two texts apart |
| `dünn` / `schmal` | near-pair | 4 / 1 | split anyway |
| `dünn` / `dicht` | look like a pair, aren't | 4 / 6 | thin paper vs. windows that seal; different scenes regardless |
| `rund` / `der Kreis` | adjective + its noun | 1 / 4 | split — a round table and a drawn circle are different objects |
| `das Holz` / `das Metall` | co-hyponyms, not synonyms | 1 / 1 | **left together on purpose.** `Holz und Metall` is attested 53 times as a coordination, and the CSV's own example is *"Möchten Sie ein Regal aus Holz oder aus Metall?"* — the choice between them is one utterance |
| `hart` / `weich`, `hell` / `dunkel`, `laut` / `leise`, `breit` / `schmal`, `senkrecht` / `waagerecht` | antonyms | see table above | left together, deliberately |

## Scene order is ownership order

The scanner records a word at its **first** sighting, so a word must not appear in any text
before the one that owns it. This batch is unusually exposed to that, because its words are
everyday adjectives that fit almost anywhere. Kept out by hand:

- **Texts 1–6 avoid `hell`, `dunkel` and `die Farbe`** — Text 7 owns all three, and a dark
  cellar or a coloured tin would have taken them in any of the six.
- **Text 1 avoids `hart`, `flach` and `das Material`**, all three of which a scene about a
  wooden table pulls in hard. The table is *schwer*, its top is measured rather than called
  flat, and nobody asks what it is made of — they say *Holz* and *Metall*.
- **Text 1 avoids `laut`** (two men and a table in a stairwell), Text 3 avoids it too
  (a hammer drill) — Text 6 owns it.
- **Text 2 avoids `das Loch`**, which is where an old wool sweater is heading; the sweater
  has a *Fleck* and nothing else. Text 3 owns `Loch`, in a wall.
- **Text 3 avoids `waagerecht`** even though a shelf is the most natural home for it in the
  whole batch. Text 4 owns it, so Bruckner says *gerade* instead.

Cast carried over unchanged from Batches 1–5, same house, now October: **Julia** (fourth
floor), **Tobi**, **Frau Wolf** (next door, widowed), **Herr Bruckner** (the caretaker),
**Jule** (Tobi's sister, the student). No new characters — this batch's words are objects,
not people, and every scene is one of the five in a room they have already been in.

---

### Scene 1 — Der Tisch im Treppenhaus
- **Premise:** Tobi has bought a table and is carrying it up to the fourth floor with Bruckner. It is stuck between the second and the third.
- **Angle:** Bruckner told him to buy a round one, and is now being proved right one centimetre at a time. Tobi's defence — that a round table has no proper sides — is delivered while the corners are what is holding them up.
- **Words (7):** die Form *(owner)*, rund *(owner)*, eckig *(owner)*, breit *(owner)*, schmal *(owner)*, das Holz *(owner)*, das Metall *(owner)*
- **Chunks:** `Holz und Metall` (53), `aus Holz sein` (545), `aus Metall sein` (236), `aus Holz bauen` (77), `die Form ändern` (88), `ein eckiger Tisch` (CSV), `ein runder Tisch` (CSV)
- **Deliberately absent:** `hart`, `flach`, `das Material` (all belong to later texts and all fit a table), `laut` (Text 6), `eng` (Text 2 — the stairs are `schmal`)
- **Glue:** *herunter-*, *vorder-*, *gesamt-*, *hinauf*, *na klar*

### Scene 2 — Sechzig Grad
- **Premise:** Julia washed Frau Wolf's wool sweater — the one Frau Wolf refused to throw away in Batch 5, Text 1 — to get a stain out. It came out of the machine child-sized and stiff.
- **Angle:** The stain really is gone. Julia holds on to that fact for four turns while the sweater visibly gets smaller in the retelling, and Frau Wolf's verdict is not about the sweater at all: it fits Julia now, so Julia can have it.
- **Words (8):** die Wolle *(owner)*, rein *(owner)*, der Stoff *(owner)*, weich *(owner)*, hart *(owner)*, glatt *(owner)*, eng *(owner)*, der Fleck *(owner)*
- **Chunks:** `aus reiner Wolle` (CSV + `reiner Stoff` 33), `ein harter Stoff` (44), `einen Fleck entfernen` (38), `einen Fleck an der Bluse haben` (80), `der Fleck stammt von` (21), `zu eng sein` (CSV)
- **Deliberately absent:** `das Loch` (Text 3), `das Material` (Text 5 — the question *"aus welchem Material?"* would be natural here and is saved)
- **Glue:** *ebenso*, *stammen*, *jedenfalls*, *ausgerechnet*, *umso*

### Scene 3 — Zwei Regale, ein Bohrer
- **Premise:** Bruckner is drilling two shelves into Julia's wall. The first went up fine. The second does not match it.
- **Angle:** Bruckner's diagnosis is that a house from 1910 has no straight walls, so the shelf is right and the building is wrong. The actual cause is on the table: a blunt pencil, and a mark three millimetres wide.
- **Words (4):** schief *(owner)*, das Loch *(owner)*, spitz *(owner)*, parallel *(owner)*
- **Chunks:** `ein Loch in der Wand` (204), `ein Loch machen` (563), `ein kleines Loch` (586), `der Bleistift ist nicht spitz` (CSV), `parallel zu` (CSV), `der Spiegel hängt schief` (CSV)
- **Deliberately absent:** `waagerecht` (Text 4 — Bruckner says *gerade*), `laut` (Text 6), `dünn` (Text 4)
- **Glue:** *ebenso*, *sämtlich-*, *daran*, *zweifellos*, *na gut*
- **Note:** the batch's thinnest scene at four targets, and deliberately so — a shelf conversation that carried eight would be the drill the skill warns about. Its work is the glue.

### Scene 4 — Der Bogen mit dem Plan
- **Premise:** The Verwaltung wants to know where each tenant's bike goes. Bruckner sits at Frau Wolf's kitchen table with the form and the courtyard plan; she has never filled one in without him.
- **Angle:** She answers every instruction with the wrong mark — a circle where a cross belongs, her name in the box for the year — and the machine that reads the sheet takes crosses only. Then she asks what the cross beside her own name means, and it is the one mark on the page nobody put there for her.
- **Words (8):** der Bogen *(owner)*, das Kreuz *(owner)*, der Kreis *(owner)*, die Linie *(owner)*, senkrecht *(owner)*, waagerecht *(owner)*, farbig *(owner)*, dünn *(owner)*
- **Chunks:** `einen Kreis malen` (35), `einen Kreis ziehen` (222), `eine gerade Linie` (131), `eine dünne Linie` (55), `ein Kreuz machen` (CSV: *"Was bedeutet dieses Kreuz hier?"*), `farbiges Papier` (CSV), `eine senkrechte Linie ziehen` (CSV)
- **Deliberately absent:** `bunt` (Text 7 — a form with coloured copies is where it would slip in), `die Farbe` (Text 7), `das Kreuz` in its religious sense
- **Glue:** *sämtlich-*, *folgend-*, *das Geschlecht*, *das Feld*, *jeweils*, *unterschreiben*
- **Note:** the fullest scene in the batch. It gets away with eight because a form is the one situation in which a second person really does dictate eight consecutive instructions.

### Scene 5 — Das Paket von Jule
- **Premise:** Jule's online order has been sitting at Julia's door since Tuesday, because Jule is never home. Julia has already opened it by the time Jule stands in the room.
- **Angle:** Everything in the parcel is a copy of something — the leather isn't, the gold isn't, and the heels the photo showed have arrived flat. Jule defends each item in turn until the only thing she can honestly praise is the box.
- **Words (6):** das Material *(owner)*, das Leder *(owner)*, das Plastik *(owner)*, künstlich *(owner)*, das Gold *(owner)*, flach *(owner)*
- **Chunks:** `aus welchem Material?` (CSV), `aus Material bestehen` (73), `echtes Leder` (65), `aus Leder sein` (86), `aus Plastik sein` (231), `Plastik und Metall` (34), `ist der Ring aus Gold?` (CSV), `flache Schuhe` (CSV)
- **Deliberately absent:** `der Kunststoff` (Text 6), `die Farbe` (Text 7), `der Stoff` (Text 2 owns it — a parcel of clothes is exactly where it would reappear first)
- **Glue:** *sogenannt-*, *heraus-*, *einschließlich*, *sich lohnen*, *zurückschicken*

### Scene 6 — Die neuen Fenster
- **Premise:** The new windows are in. Frau Wolf has complained about the street for eleven years and now cannot sleep.
- **Angle:** She gets exactly what she asked for and wants it undone: the silence is the problem. Bruckner, who fought the Verwaltung for two years to get the windows approved, has to listen to her ask for the old ones back.
- **Words (4):** der Kunststoff *(owner)*, dicht *(owner)*, laut *(owner)*, leise *(owner)*
- **Chunks:** `aus Kunststoff sein` (30), `die Fenster sind nicht dicht` (CSV), `zu laut sein` (CSV), `leiser stellen` (CSV), `seid leise` (CSV)
- **Deliberately absent:** `das Plastik` (Text 5 — the window frames are `Kunststoff` and the word `Plastik` never appears here), `hell`/`dunkel` (Text 7)
- **Glue:** *einschließlich*, *gleichfalls*, *seitdem*, *kaum*, *sich beschweren*

### Scene 7 — Die Farbe für den Flur
- **Premise:** Julia and Tobi are painting her hallway. Julia bought the tin without looking at the wall; the hallway has no window.
- **Angle:** Tobi is proved right after exactly one wall, and Frau Wolf arrives with the only suggestion worse than the dark one. Julia's argument that the colour dries lighter is the thing every person who has bought the wrong tin says.
- **Words (4):** die Farbe *(owner)*, dunkel *(owner)*, hell *(owner)*, bunt *(owner)*
- **Chunks:** `Farbe und Form` (249), `diese Farbe trocknet schnell` (CSV), `welche Farbe magst du?` (CSV), `es wird hell` (CSV), `ein buntes Kleid` (CSV), `dunkle Haare` (CSV)
- **Glue:** *Spezial-*, *ohnehin*, *zugeben*, *dermaßen*, *bereits*
- **Note:** the last text owns the three words every earlier text had to avoid, which is why it is last.
