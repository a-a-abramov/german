# Shared Glue Pool — function words woven through every batch

**What this is.** The Goethe B1 list contains ~326 closed-class function words with no depictable referent — prepositions, conjunctions, pronouns, determiners, particles, question words, directional adverbs, modal/auxiliary verbs. You cannot draw *zwischen*, *obwohl*, *derselbe*, or *ziemlich* the way you can draw a heater or a cat. Forcing standalone scenes out of them produces exactly the un-imaginable texts we want to avoid.

So they are **not** scene-batches. They are a shared pool the text-writer draws from **while writing every other topic's texts**, as connective tissue. A glue word is 'covered' the first time it appears in any finalized text — anywhere.

The dividing line is **grammatical class, not abstractness**: every open-class word stays in a scene, even abstract ones (`die Meinung`, `entscheiden`, `wichtig`, `zweifeln` are grounded via situations — that's the whole method). Only closed-class operators pool.

---

## THE RULE

> **It's OKAY to repeat these words. It's NOT okay to leave them out.**

Repetition across many texts is expected and good — that's how glue words actually get learned (in varied context, many times). The only failure mode is a word that never appears in *any* text.

---

## How the writer uses this pool

1. **Pass 1 (natural):** when writing each topic's texts, pull in whatever pool words fit naturally. Don't force them — but don't avoid them either. Tick each word below the first time it lands in a finalized text.

2. **Pass 2 (targeted):** after the concrete batches are drafted, list the still-unticked words and deliberately route them into upcoming or revised texts where they fit.

3. **Pass 3 (sweep):** any stragglers get a purpose-built home — folded into an existing scene, or a short dedicated 'glue' text per stage if a handful truly resist placement. Goal: **all ticked.**


**Coverage target:** every one of the **326** words below used at least once across the corpus. Track with the checkboxes (`☐`→`☑`).
